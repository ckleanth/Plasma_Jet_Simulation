The device

Helium, a glass capillary and a pulsed ring electrode

A cold atmospheric-pressure plasma jet blows helium through a thin glass capillary into the open air. A pulsed high voltage on a ring electrode around the capillary drives the discharge: free electrons gain energy in the electric field and ionise the gas, so their number multiplies. In experiments, a front of ionisation, a "plasma bullet", then leaves the tube and travels to the target at about 10 km/s, while the gas itself flows at about 50 m/s. Such jets are studied for treating surfaces, materials and living tissue without heating them.

This work simulates such a jet in full, following the plasma model of a doctoral thesis from the University of Cyprus (Lazarou, 2019). It first solves the gas flow, helium mixing with air on its way to a dielectric target, and then the plasma on top of it: 16 species, their chemistry and the electric field in the gas, the glass and the target. The pictures show the first phase of a voltage pulse: as the electrode voltage rises, electrons multiply inside the capillary, just below the electrode.

What it demonstrates

Four capabilities in one simulation

Coupled physics

Gas flow and plasma

The helium–air jet is solved first. The plasma model then runs on the computed flow and gas mixture, from inside the capillary to the target.

Solver development

A plasma model inside CFX

Ansys CFX has no plasma model of its own. Custom User Fortran routines were developed for it to carry the charged species and their coupling to the electric field.

Fields through materials

Gas, glass and target

The electric potential is solved through the gas, the glass capillary and the dielectric target, and charge collects on the dielectric surfaces.

Cross-checking

Two independent codes

A second model of the same equations, built in OpenFOAM, runs on the same mesh and finds the same ionised region.

Ionisation

Watching the plasma start

The time-lapse has one frame for each hour of computation, from 0.15 to 4.88 µs into the voltage pulse. The seed electrons first fade; then, as the electrode voltage rises, electrons multiply below the ring electrode. Between 4.01 and 4.88 µs the voltage rises from 2.20 to 2.67 kV, and the peak electron density from 4.2×1013 to 2.5×1017 per m3.

Status on 23 November 2026. The CFX simulation had made about 31,000 time steps and reached 4.88 µs, two thirds of the way up the 7.3 µs voltage rise. The plasma had not yet left the capillary. The run continues.

Checks

Two independent codes, one plasma

In CFX the plasma runs in custom User Fortran routines on four processor cores, with a time step set by the ionisation rate.

A second model of the same equations, built on the ltpFoam solver of OpenFOAM v1912, runs on the same mesh. At 3.80 µs both codes show the same ionised region below the electrode, with peak electron densities of 1.43×1013 per m3 in CFX and 1.27×1013 per m3 in OpenFOAM.

The structured mesh has 25,024 cells in the axisymmetric section, graded to 10 µm at the walls.

  • Device. The thesis's experiment: a capillary of 0.9 mm inner and 1.35 mm outer diameter, a copper-tape electrode 1.5 mm from the exit and a fused-quartz target 2 mm from the exit. The relative permittivities are 4.2 for the glass and 8 for the target.
  • Gas flow. Laminar, up to 52 m/s on the axis, with helium mixing into the surrounding air.
  • Plasma. A drift–diffusion fluid model with an electron energy equation and 16 species: electrons; the ions He+, He2+, N2+, N4+, O2+, O4+, O- and O2-; four excited states of helium atoms and molecules; and the atoms N and O, and ozone.
  • Field. The electric potential is solved in the gas, the glass and the target, and charge collects on the dielectric surfaces.
  • Electron data. Transport and rate coefficients from the Boltzmann solver BOLSIG+, with cross sections from LXCat.

Built step by step

Before the jet: a barrier discharge

The model was built up on simpler cases first: the gas flow of a simpler helium jet, an electron avalanche checked against its analytic solution, and a parallel-plate dielectric barrier discharge in helium, also run in both codes. The barrier discharge shows the structure that a discharge model must resolve: a thin cathode fall, with a strong field and energetic electrons, beside a quasi-neutral plasma.
